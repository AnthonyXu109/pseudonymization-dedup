## E6. Diff exposure of missed identifiers under FAKER-DOC
Data: TAB, the 1,268 originals and 400 paragraph copies of Table IV. Release: FAKER-DOC applied separately to every
document with the spaCy en_core_web_lg detector spans (results/tab_detected.pkl), as a holder without gold annotations
would release. Ground truth: gold first-annotator spans (build_corpus.build()).

Name-like token: a token matching [A-Z][a-z]+ (at least two letters) that is not at the start of a line or after
sentence punctuation. Leaked token of a document: a name-like token of the raw text that lies inside a gold span of type
PERSON, ORG or LOC and overlaps no detector span (so it is released verbatim). Evaluation is at the level of unique token
strings per document.

Attacker: holds the released corpus and a pair (released original, released copy) of the same record. Candidate tokens
in the released original: name-like tokens whose document frequency in the released corpus (all 1,668 documents) is at
most 5. Two rules:
- one copy: flag every candidate token of the released original;
- two copies: flag candidate tokens of the released original that also occur in the released copy.
Metrics over the 400 pairs, pooled: precision (flagged strings that are leaked strings of the original), recall
(leaked strings of the original that also occur in the raw copy and are flagged), and the number of surrogate strings
flagged (strings introduced by FAKER-DOC). Secondary: the same without the frequency filter.
Expectation stated before running: two-copy precision much higher than one-copy precision, recall similar.
Also reported: how many of the 400 released pairs still have exact word-5-gram Jaccard >= 0.3, i.e. how easy the pair is
to find.

Output: results/v10/e6_diff.json. Script: exp_v10.py.
